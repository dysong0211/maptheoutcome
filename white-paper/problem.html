White Paper / Understand MatOc

The problem MatOc solves

Why evidence reconstruction can separate a programme result from the decisions and changes behind it.

Programme teams can produce a large volume of documentation without leaving a clear, connected explanation of their work. A progress report may contain an emerging result, a meeting note may explain an adaptation and a spreadsheet may hold a relevant indicator. A later reader has to discover and connect them.

The reconstruction problem

When reporting or evaluation begins, staff may need to locate source material, reconcile versions, ask colleagues what a change meant and repeat explanations that were already given elsewhere. If people have moved on, the reasoning behind a decision may be harder to recover than the decision itself.

This is the operational problem MatOc targets. The product does not assume that all programmes are poorly planned or that missing public data means no work was done. It focuses on a narrower question: can teams preserve a more usable account of implementation without creating another substantial reporting obligation?

Four things can become separated

  • The intended change: what the programme expected to achieve and for whom.
  • What happened: delivery observations, reported results and relevant feedback.
  • Why the approach changed: constraints, decisions, risks and adaptations.
  • What the evidence supports: the source, its coverage, uncertainty and competing explanations.

What a better starting point looks like

An evaluator should be able to inspect a dated, source-linked history and see where evidence is missing or disputed. Programme teams should be able to reuse approved material while still interpreting and validating each report for its audience.

The ambition is a stronger starting point for judgement. It is not to remove investigation, guarantee complete coverage or automate an explanation that the evidence cannot support. Background studies and public-record research are presented separately in the Research collection and the homepage's The Need section.
