White Paper / Understand MatOc

What MatOc does

Capture outcome-relevant evidence, preserve context and support reuse across reporting and learning.

MatOc is designed to turn routine programme documentation into a structured evidence record. The important unit is an inspectable item of evidence, connected to its source and context.

Organise what already exists

A team contributes a relevant update or document. The proposed system identifies candidate results, decisions, material changes, risks, lessons and unanswered questions. It should preserve the distinction between something a source reports and something the programme has established.

For example, “attendance seems to have improved after outreach moved to Saturdays” is a reported signal. It is not yet proof that the schedule change caused the improvement. A reviewer should be able to preserve the signal and identify what further evidence is needed.

Keep the explanation with the result

Approved items are intended to connect to programme objectives, dates, places and related records. A decision to change delivery can remain linked to the problem that prompted it and to later observations. Supporting and contradicting evidence should both remain visible.

Reuse the record

The planned record can support donor questions, report preparation, handover, evaluation and future programme design. Reuse means finding and citing approved evidence; it does not mean submitting the same interpretation to every audience without review.

What often happens now What MatOc is designed to make possible
Teams reconstruct answers from scattered documents Evidence is organised as updates are contributed
Results lose the context behind them Decisions, risks and changes remain connected
Lessons are hard to find after staff changes A shared programme history supports handover
Claims become detached from their sources Approved items preserve source attribution

Keep the scope useful

MatOc should capture the road to the outcome, rather than every operational step. Task assignment, timesheets, scheduling and general file storage remain outside the core proposition. The test is whether an item helps someone understand an outcome, a consequential change or an evidence gap.
