White Paper / Building MatOc

Vision and roadmap

Build programme memory first. Make evidence reusable across reporting, evaluation and future programmes.

MatOc's vision is for every programme to leave a usable account of what it tried, what happened, what changed and what the team learned. That account should grow during implementation, remain connected to its sources and help the next person understand the work.

The vision

The long-term aim is a reporting and learning system that connects resources, activities and documented outcomes without requiring staff to reconstruct the programme each time someone asks a question. A programme record should be useful while decisions are still open, not only when a final report is due.

Across an organisation, these records could make it easier to find relevant experience from earlier programmes. A useful network would preserve differences between contexts as carefully as it reveals similarities. A connection between two records is a starting point for investigation, not proof that an intervention will transfer successfully.

The roadmap

StageWhat we are building towardsWhat must be established
1. Capture and reviewSource-linked proposals, a review inbox and an approved programme record.Reviewers can check useful evidence without losing context or adding excessive work.
2. Connected workflowAuthorised shared-folder connections, file-change detection and review digests.Sources stay in scope, access is respected and duplicate or changed files are handled reliably.
3. Reporting supportEvidence linked to outcomes and draft report exports with source citations.Teams can reuse approved material and produce an accurate final report with less total effort.
4. Portfolio learningSearch across permitted programmes, relevant lessons and recurring evidence gaps.Definitions, context and permissions are strong enough for meaningful comparisons.

This is a proposed sequence, not a dated release commitment. MatOc remains in development. Pilot results will determine priorities and whether a capability is ready to expand.

What the first pilots should test

A pilot should begin with a defined programme, a limited source collection and named reviewers. It should examine the whole workflow: connecting sources, handling proposals, checking citations, retrieving evidence and preparing a report. A faster export is not a net saving if review and maintenance take more time than they remove.

Alongside workload, pilots should test whether a reader can understand a significant implementation decision from the record, whether uncertainty survives extraction, and whether evidence remains accessible at handover. These are practical tests of programme memory.

From one programme to a network

Expansion should start only when individual records are useful. Future teams could search for a delivery constraint, an adaptation or a lesson and see the evidence behind it. Cross-programme discovery must retain the location, population, timing, resources and limitations that affect relevance.

Sharing would be deliberate and permission-based. A lesson may be suitable for wider use even when its original source contains information that must stay restricted. The homepage network remains an illustration of this vision, not a live set of participating organisations.

The intended impact

The ambition is less repeated evidence assembly, clearer outcome explanations and learning that survives staff changes and funding cycles. Better records can support evaluation and decisions; they cannot supply missing funding, guarantee complete evidence or establish causality on their own. Public claims about MatOc's impact should follow measured pilot results.
