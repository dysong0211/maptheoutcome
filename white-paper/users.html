Who uses MatOc

Roles for programme teams, monitoring and evaluation specialists, evaluators and funders.

MatOc is being designed for organisations that implement, fund or assess programmes and need to retain the evidence behind their work. Its first practical use case is a team maintaining a shared record for one programme.

Programme teams

Programme managers and implementation staff contribute material through a workflow agreed with their organisation. They should be able to review proposed evidence without rewriting every update. The intended benefit is a usable programme history that supports current decisions as well as reporting later.

Monitoring, evaluation and learning specialists

M&E specialists help define what counts as useful evidence, how items relate to intended outcomes and how uncertainty should be recorded. They can review whether an approved statement is supported and identify gaps that need investigation. The evidence record should complement the indicators and systems the organisation already uses.

Evaluators and reviewers

An evaluator can use a shared, permitted record to orient an investigation: which decisions mattered, what changed, which sources are available and where accounts disagree. Access to the record does not make the evaluator dependent on the team's interpretation. Independent corroboration and assessment remain necessary.

Funders and portfolio teams

Funders may need a permitted view of how implementation and documented results connect. Portfolio teams may later look for comparable lessons across programmes. Neither use case implies unrestricted access to confidential source material or automatic publication of internal evidence.

Who owns the work?

Each pilot should identify a programme owner, contributors, reviewers and intended readers. The organisation must decide who can approve an item, who can change an interpretation and which records can be shared. MatOc's role is to support that accountability, not to invent authority for the software or its AI model.
