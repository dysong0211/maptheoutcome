WHITE PAPER

Overview

Map the Outcome is a programme intelligence system being built to help organisations preserve the evidence behind their work as it happens.

It uses AI to organise the updates teams already produce, including reports, meeting notes, field updates, risks, decisions, lessons and results. Human reviewers approve what enters the programme record, and every approved item links back to its source.

MtO does not replace evaluation, project management or monitoring systems. It gives them a stronger starting point: a source-linked record of what happened, what changed, why decisions were made, what risks affected delivery, and what evidence supports the final outcome.

What MtO is built to improve
Faster reportingEvidence is already organised when reporting starts.
Stronger traceabilityApproved claims connect back to source material.
Institutional memoryDecisions, adaptations and lessons stay with the programme.
Clearer learningFuture teams can see what changed and why.
01Problem

Programme evidence is created during the work. The explanation is often rebuilt at the end.

Teams produce updates, field reports, meeting notes, spreadsheets and donor reports throughout implementation. But the material that explains the final outcome is often scattered across systems and people.

The gap MtO addresses

The outcome story is not usually missing because no one did the work. It is thin because decisions, risks, adaptations and lessons are not captured as one connected record while the work is unfolding.

02What MtO does

From messy programme updates to structured evidence.

The input already exists. Map the Outcome gives it structure, attribution and relationships.

What the team writes
“Attendance seems to have improved after moving outreach sessions to Saturdays. Still having problems with stock at two facilities and the team is discussing redistribution.”
→
What MtO proposes for review
Result signal

Attendance may have improved after the schedule change.

Decision

Outreach moved to Saturdays.

Risk

Stock shortages at two facilities.

Evidence

CHW feedback + district meeting.

03How it works
01

Write

Teams keep producing normal updates, meeting notes, field reports and programme documents.

02

Extract

AI proposes outcomes, risks, decisions, changes, lessons, evidence gaps and questions.

03

Review

People approve, edit or reject proposed evidence before it enters the record.

04

Connect

Approved evidence links to outcomes, sources, locations, decisions and related records.

05

Use

Teams use the record for reporting, evaluation preparation, handover and future design.

06Impact thesis

The impact MtO is built to test.

MtO is not yet claiming proven sector-wide savings or better outcomes. The pilot should test whether a reviewed evidence record reduces repeated source-finding, improves traceability and preserves useful lessons.

Less reconstructionMeasure time spent locating and reassembling evidence.
Better traceabilityMeasure whether approved claims lead back to source material.
Stronger handoverMeasure whether new team members can understand decisions and risks faster.
Reusable learningMeasure whether lessons from one programme inform the next.
07Investors and partners

Why this can matter commercially and institutionally.

Development organisations, governments and foundations already spend time producing reports, preparing evaluations and preserving accountability. MtO focuses on the reusable evidence layer underneath those tasks.

What a pilot should prove

Adoption by real teams, useful extraction accuracy, lower repeated evidence assembly, trusted source links, clear permissions, and a value case that survives comparison with existing systems.

08Future plans
The long-term vision

A searchable evidence network across programmes.

Start with one programme record. Over time, connect lessons across countries, districts, interventions and funding cycles so future programme design can begin with the evidence that previous teams already created.
