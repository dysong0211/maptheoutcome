What happens to programme memory when the team moves on?

The handover should carry the reasons, not just the files.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Continuity includes decisions, changed assumptions and unfinished responsibilities.
  • A new reader needs context and usable source links.
  • Access after programme closure needs an explicit arrangement.

A useful final report should allow a new reader to understand both what was achieved and what remains unresolved. Programme memory includes the reasons behind decisions, the assumptions that changed and the responsibilities that continue after a grant ends.

The UN's knowledge-management review describes losses when expertise leaves and difficulties finding knowledge across fragmented repositories. Separately, GEF's portfolio assessment distinguishes satisfactory outcomes at completion from the likelihood that benefits will endure. Neither finding supports a claim that staff stop caring when programmes close. UN knowledge-management review, GEF portfolio assessment

My proposed standard for programme handover is simple: another person should be able to establish what the team tried, what changed, what evidence was available and what still needs attention.

That means retaining more than a list of achievements. A useful handover might explain why a delivery model changed, which partner will maintain a service, which risk remains open and when a promising result needs another check.

MatOc is being built towards this kind of continuity. Its programme record should accumulate during implementation, with people reviewing the proposed evidence and preserving links to sources.

The system also needs an end-of-programme arrangement. Who can access the record after funding ends? Who can correct it? Which sensitive material should be restricted? What evidence must remain available independently of a subscription?

Those questions are part of preserving institutional memory. A record has lasting value when the next team can understand it, trust its provenance and use it responsibly.
