What if the outcome arrives after the grant ends?

The funding timeline and the timeline of change may differ.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Closure does not answer every question about durability.
  • Longer-term follow-up needs responsibilities and resources.
  • A programme record should show what remains unknown.

A funding period defines when particular activities and obligations are supported. The question of what those activities changed may require a different observation period.

Hamory and colleagues studied economic outcomes up to twenty years after a randomized school deworming intervention in Kenya. Their findings illustrate the value of sustained follow-up in that setting. They should not be treated as a prediction for every health intervention or a reason to delay all evaluation until decades have passed. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 2021

For programme design, the practical issue is which questions can reasonably be answered by the planned closing date. A team may be able to report delivery and early uptake, while durability or longer-term effects remain open.

I would want the closing record to distinguish these questions. What was observed? What is still expected? Which result needs follow-up, when would that follow-up be informative, and who would be responsible for it?

Imagine a programme supporting a new service model. At close, the team can document implementation and early use. Whether the service remains available after external support ends may need a later check. The first report can be complete about the period it covers without pretending to settle the second question.

MatOc's programme record could preserve that distinction and the evidence needed for future inquiry. Longitudinal research would still require its own design, resources and permissions.

A responsible account of programme outcomes should make the time horizon visible. It should explain both what the evidence can establish today and what remains worth learning later.
