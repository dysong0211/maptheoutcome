The report is finished. Can we explain the result?

A completed report is not always a usable programme history.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Documents can answer separate questions while leaving their connections unclear.
  • A useful record preserves uncertainty as well as achievements.
  • Test whether a new reader can find and assess the evidence.

Finishing a report should leave an organisation better able to explain its work. A useful test is what the next reader can establish: what changed, which evidence supports that conclusion, and what remains uncertain.

Public research suggests those questions can remain difficult. The 2024 Aid Transparency Index found that only about a third of its 50 assessed organisations published good, current results. Separately, the UN's 2025 inspection found widespread capacity pressures from donor reporting and oversight requests. These studies examine different populations and measures; together, they suggest a problem worth investigating between reporting effort and usable evidence. Aid Transparency Index, UN JIU review

Consider an illustrative programme with a complete quarterly report, an indicator spreadsheet and a folder of meeting notes. The report says delivery improved. The spreadsheet shows attendance. The meeting notes contain the explanation for a change in approach. Each document may serve its original purpose, while the connection between them still requires someone to reconstruct it.

That connection is central to Map the Outcome's proposed role. MatOc is being built to associate reviewed evidence with intended outcomes, decisions and original sources as implementation continues.

A useful record should also retain complications: disagreement, incomplete coverage and results that did not follow the plan. Otherwise, a more polished account could simply make uncertainty harder to see.

The practical question for a pilot is whether someone unfamiliar with the programme can find and assess the evidence more easily. A completed report is an output of the workflow. A usable programme history is the capability MatOc needs to demonstrate.
