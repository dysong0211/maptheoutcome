Are we documenting the programme we actually delivered?

When delivery changes, the programme description needs a history.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • The implemented approach may differ from the original plan.
  • Descriptions should preserve significant adaptations and their reasons.
  • Evaluation needs a clear account of what was actually delivered.

Imagine an intervention introduced with a particular training format, staffing model and referral process. During implementation, the team adjusts all three. An evaluation needs to know which version participants actually experienced.

The FRAME paper provides a structured way to document adaptations, including their timing, what changed, who made the decision and the reasons behind it. It is a framework for describing modifications and supporting research, not evidence that recording a modification makes it effective. Wiltsey Stirman, Baumann and Miller, 2019

This is closely related to the programme memory I want MatOc to support. A material change should remain connected to the original design, the evidence or constraint that prompted it and what was observed afterwards.

The record also needs to leave room for interpretation. Some changes may preserve the intended mechanism while improving local fit. Others may remove an element that mattered. Recording what happened enables that question to be examined; it does not answer it automatically.

For a team preparing an evaluation, the distinction is practical. A description copied from the original proposal may no longer describe the intervention under review. The implementation history should make important differences visible.

MatOc should focus on changes that help explain delivery and outcomes, with human judgement about relevance. The aim is to produce an account another person can understand without asking the team to narrate every operational adjustment.

A useful record should let the evaluator establish what was implemented before drawing conclusions about why it produced the observed result.
