Who is missing from the programme story?

An average can describe progress while leaving someone out.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • A programme summary should make coverage visible.
  • Missing voices create questions rather than automatic conclusions.
  • Disaggregation and source context help focus follow-up.

Imagine a programme report that says stakeholders were consulted. The statement may be accurate while leaving important questions unanswered. Who attended? Who could not attend? Which concerns reached the final decision? What happened when people disagreed?

Mansuri and Rao's World Bank research synthesis examines how context, power and collective action shape participatory development. It identifies weaknesses in attention to context and in monitoring arrangements that support learning. Its argument is more complicated than either assuming participation works or dismissing it. Localizing Development: Does Participation Work?

A programme record should help a reader understand whose experience informed the account. That requires care with both representation and confidentiality. A meeting note should not be made public merely because it contains useful feedback. Nor should a summary imply that everyone agreed when the source describes a narrower set of views.

I would want evidence review to retain these boundaries. A concern raised by a local partner should remain attributable at an appropriate level. A finding from a particular group should keep that scope. An absence of feedback should remain an absence, rather than becoming a statement of satisfaction.

For MatOc, this is a reason to preserve context around evidence and allow reviewers to flag missing perspectives. The system cannot infer what unrepresented people would have said.

A more complete programme story begins with being honest about the people it can speak for. That honesty gives the team a practical next step: identify whose experience needs further attention and decide how to hear it responsibly.
