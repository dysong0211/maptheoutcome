How should local knowledge sit alongside published research?

Keep the source and the reasoning behind both kinds of advice.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Preferences for locally relevant evidence do not establish its correctness.
  • Local advice should retain attribution and context.
  • Disagreement can identify the next useful information to collect.

A published study can estimate what happened under particular conditions. A person working locally may understand a delivery constraint that the study never encountered. A useful programme record should make room to examine both contributions.

Vivalt, Coville and KC used a choice experiment with policy professionals attending World Bank and Inter-American Development Bank workshops. Participants placed substantial weight on local expert recommendations and locally relevant evidence. The choices were hypothetical: the experiment does not establish that local experts were correct or that those preferences improved programme outcomes. Author manuscript, 2024

For programme intelligence, I would want local advice to retain its attribution and reasoning. “The district team recommended a different delivery model” is a starting point. What constraint did they identify? What experience informed their judgement? Which parts of the recommendation can be checked?

Formal evidence deserves similar treatment. Where was it generated? Who implemented the intervention? How closely do the conditions match the programme being considered?

MatOc should help people bring those questions together. It could preserve a local observation alongside a relevant study without automatically treating either as the final answer. Disagreement would remain visible, with an explanation of what additional information could resolve it.

The ambition is a record that respects knowledge enough to make it inspectable. Local expertise should remain connected to the person, place and circumstances that give it meaning. Research should retain the boundaries that determine how confidently it can inform a different setting.
