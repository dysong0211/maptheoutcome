Do we question good news as carefully as bad news?

A welcome result still needs its uncertainty and competing evidence.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • A cited study examines how its participants updated their beliefs.
  • Its findings should not be generalised to every decision-maker.
  • Useful review keeps supporting and conflicting evidence visible.

Imagine two updates arriving on the same day. One reports encouraging uptake. The other suggests that a less visible group is struggling to participate. A useful review process should give both observations a fair opportunity to change the programme's understanding.

In experiments involving policymakers, practitioners and researchers, Vivalt and Coville found that participants updated more in response to favourable findings and were relatively insensitive to confidence intervals. They did not find significant differences in these biases between the professional groups. The study concerns its recruited participants, not every person making development decisions. Vivalt and Coville, 2023

The product implication I draw is that evidence review should deliberately make uncertainty and disagreement easy to find. A promising result deserves questions about its source, coverage and alternative explanations. A disappointing finding deserves the same care rather than immediate dismissal as an implementation anomaly.

For MatOc, an outcome record should have space for supporting evidence, conflicting evidence and unresolved interpretation. Approval should establish what a source supports, not whether its message is welcome.

I would also preserve a short account of what changed the team's view. Did the new information alter a decision, reduce confidence or prompt further investigation? That history would make learning more concrete than a retrospective statement that the team remained flexible.

A credible programme story can contain progress and uncertainty at the same time. Giving both a place in the record is a practical standard for the system I want MatOc to become.
