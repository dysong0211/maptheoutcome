Why wait until the end to examine implementation barriers?

A review of 34 studies raises a question about when explanations become useful.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • A 2020 review found 59% of its included studies used CFIR at endline.
  • That finding concerns one framework’s reviewed applications, not all evaluation.
  • Earlier capture is useful only if someone can act on the information.

An explanation of implementation problems has particular value when someone can still act on it. The same observation may support a practical adjustment during delivery and a recommendation after the programme ends.

A 2020 systematic review examined 34 studies using the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research in 21 low- and middle-income countries. It found that 59% applied the framework at study endline, primarily for analysis or interpretation. That is a finding about the reviewed applications of one framework, not all programme evaluation. Systematic review, Implementation Science, 2020

The finding raises a useful design question: what would a team need to recognise an implementation barrier earlier? A framework alone does not answer it. Someone must notice the issue, make the observation available and have responsibility for deciding what to do.

Consider a hypothetical training programme where attendance is adequate but participants cannot use the new process because the required equipment is unavailable. Recording attendance remains useful. Recording the equipment constraint could support a different decision about procurement, sequencing or the training itself.

For MatOc, earlier capture should mean keeping relevant observations available for review while implementation continues. It should not mean asking staff to complete an exhaustive diagnostic assessment every week.

A sensible pilot would select a few important barriers and examine whether the record helped the team recognise and respond to them. The test is not simply whether an explanation was documented sooner. It is whether earlier access made the explanation useful to someone with an opportunity to act.
