Why do teams keep rebuilding the same answer?

Different requests. Overlapping evidence. Where could the work be reused?

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Reporting volume alone does not establish that reports are unnecessary.
  • A simpler template does not remove every source-finding task.
  • Measure total effort, including review and correction.

Imagine a programme team answering the same underlying question for a donor update, a management review, a partner meeting and an evaluation request. The audiences differ, but the source material overlaps. The opportunity for improvement lies in how much of that work can be reused safely.

The scale of reporting is documented. A UN inspection recorded 3,000 WHO donor reports for 2016. That is a historical count, not a judgement that all those reports were unnecessary. A later harmonisation pilot found mixed results: some feedback indicated time savings, while other reports took the same time or longer. JIU, Annex II, GPPi's 2018 pilot review

The lesson for a product team is practical. A simpler template may help, but the work before the template also matters: finding the right source, checking its date, resolving conflicting versions and asking a colleague what a sentence meant.

MatOc's proposed approach is to preserve reviewed evidence with its source and programme context, so subsequent requests can start from material already checked. The team would still decide which evidence answers the question and whether it remains current.

Reuse must also preserve boundaries. A record approved for one purpose may need updated context or different access permissions for another. Repeating a claim without checking its relevance would create a different kind of waste.

The measure of success should therefore be total effort per accepted response, including review and correction. MatOc should earn its place by making that work easier while maintaining the quality of the answer.
