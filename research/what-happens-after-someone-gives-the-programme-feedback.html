What happens after someone gives the programme feedback?

The feedback was collected. Who acted, and what changed?

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Collecting feedback is only one step in a response.
  • The record should connect a concern to responsibility and follow-up.
  • A useful account makes unresolved issues visible.

Collecting feedback is an invitation to explain what will happen next. A useful process should make it possible to follow a concern from the person who raised it to a response, a decision or an explicit explanation that it remains unresolved.

Research in Uganda offers a specific example of why the design of an accountability process matters. Björkman Nyqvist, de Walque and Svensson found sustained benefits from a community-monitoring intervention combining participation with performance information. A lower-cost intervention encouraging participation without that information did not improve care quality or health outcomes. These findings concern those interventions and settings. American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, 2017

The broader principle I draw is that recording a concern should be connected to a usable response mechanism. A programme team needs to know who can investigate, which evidence would help and how the person raising the issue will hear back.

Imagine a participant reporting that a service is regularly unavailable. Logging the message is one step. Checking the pattern, identifying responsibility and explaining the response are separate steps that deserve a place in the record.

MatOc could preserve those connections without becoming a complaints-management system. Where an organisation already has a formal channel, the evidence record should point to the authorised process and retain only the information appropriate for programme learning.

The question for a reviewer would then be concrete: was the concern heard, what was done with it and what changed afterwards? That is a useful standard against which to assess feedback practice.
