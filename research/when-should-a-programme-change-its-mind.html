When should a programme change its mind?

The outreach schedule changed. Keep the reason, then check what followed.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • A plan contains assumptions that implementation can challenge.
  • An explained adaptation can strengthen accountability.
  • A record should leave room to discover that a change did not help.

A programme plan is a set of commitments built around an explanation of how change might happen. I would want a team to take those commitments seriously while remaining willing to question the explanation.

Imagine an outreach programme designed around weekday visits. Early feedback suggests the intended participants are unavailable during those hours. The team changes its schedule. The important questions are what prompted the decision, whether the change reached different people and what the evidence showed afterwards.

The proposal for Problem-Driven Iterative Adaptation developed by Andrews, Pritchett and Woolcock emphasises locally defined problems, experimentation and feedback. It offers an approach to institutional reform; it is not an experiment demonstrating that every programme benefits from constant adjustment. Andrews, Pritchett and Woolcock, 2012

My view is that a well-explained change should strengthen a programme's accountability. A reader should be able to see what the original plan expected, what the team encountered, which options it considered and why the revised approach was reasonable at the time.

That also leaves room to discover that a change did not help. A record built only around defending decisions would be a poor learning tool.

For MatOc, this means preserving revisions and their reasons. An updated plan should remain connected to the earlier assumption and the evidence that challenged it. The record can then support a more useful conversation: did the team recognise the problem, respond appropriately and learn from what followed?

That is the kind of programme memory I would want to carry into the next funding cycle.
