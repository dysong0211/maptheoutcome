What evidence would make us change course?

Agree on the questions before the programme needs an answer.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Expectations can identify assumptions worth monitoring.
  • Revisions should retain the reasoning that preceded them.
  • A useful record connects observations to decisions still open.

Before a programme begins, I would ask one question alongside the objectives: what evidence would make us reconsider this approach?

The answer does not need to be a rigid rule. It could identify a practical concern, an assumption that needs checking or a condition under which the planned delivery model becomes unsuitable.

Imagine a team introducing a digital reporting tool. It expects the tool to simplify work at facilities. Before implementation, the team could agree to investigate if staff still need to enter the same information into several systems, if certain facilities cannot submit records, or if correcting data requires more effort than anticipated.

Those questions give the team something specific to learn. They also make the original reasoning available when the project is reviewed. A later decision to revise the tool can be assessed against what people knew and intended at the time.

I would preserve these expectations in the programme record and revisit them at meaningful points. They should be easy to amend when the context changes, with the earlier version still available.

This is a product principle I would like MatOc to support: connect an intended outcome to the assumptions that deserve attention. Then link relevant observations to those assumptions as updates arrive.

It would help distinguish a deliberate adaptation from a change explained only afterwards. It would also make success more informative. If the approach works, the record could show which expectations were supported and which turned out to matter less than the team thought.
