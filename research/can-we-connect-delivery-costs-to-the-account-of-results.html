Can we connect delivery costs to the account of results?

A spending total does not explain what it took to deliver.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Costs and implementation details help readers interpret results.
  • Comparisons need consistent definitions and boundaries.
  • A record can support the questions without settling value-for-money by itself.

If an organisation wants to decide whether to repeat an intervention, I would want its account of results to include what delivery required.

Imagine two programmes reporting similar uptake. One used routine staff time and existing facilities. The other relied on substantial specialist support and temporary delivery arrangements. Before comparing them, a reviewer would need to understand those differences and decide which costs belong in the comparison.

A programme record could help preserve the inputs behind that judgement: staff roles, partner contributions, equipment, support arrangements and material changes in the delivery model. It should distinguish actual expenditure from estimates and identify whose resources are included.

The same discipline applies to claims that a new workflow saves money. Time released for another task is useful capacity, but a cash-saving claim requires evidence that expenditure changes. The price of software is also only one part of the resources required to introduce and operate it.

For MatOc, I would begin with links to the authorised financial and operational records that help explain implementation. That would support later costing work without pretending that a collection of documents is already a cost-effectiveness analysis.

A proper comparison still needs a defined perspective, comparable outcomes, a time period and transparent assumptions. Those choices should remain visible to the reader.

The ambition is an evidence base that makes the delivery model easier to understand. When someone considers repeating or expanding the programme, they should be able to examine both the reported result and the resources that helped produce it.

About this perspective

This is a product perspective or illustrative discussion, not an empirical finding. Its recommendations are propositions for readers to consider and test.
