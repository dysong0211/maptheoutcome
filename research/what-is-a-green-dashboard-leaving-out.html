What is a green dashboard leaving out?

Everything is on schedule. Which important question is still unanswered?

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Delivery status can leave uncertainty and constraints out of view.
  • Concerns should remain visible even when milestones are achieved.
  • The underlying record should help a reviewer ask the next question.

Imagine a programme dashboard with every activity marked green. Training happened on schedule. Materials were distributed. Spending followed the budget. A reader still needs to know whether the intended participants could use what was delivered and whether an important assumption has changed.

This is a question about what management asks people to pay attention to. Dan Honig's research argues that tight controls on field staff can undermine performance, particularly in unpredictable environments. The paper examines foreign aid implementation; its finding should not be turned into a claim that monitoring is generally harmful. Honig, International Organization, 2019

I would design a programme review to ask for both delivery information and consequential exceptions. Which concern needs a decision? Which result is less certain than it appears? Where has local judgement changed the approach?

A green status should leave those questions open. It should also be possible to report a problem without first turning it into a success story. Otherwise, the status label may tell a manager very little about the work that deserves attention.

The implication for MatOc is practical: preserve observations that complicate the summary. A delivery milestone, a staff concern and a contradictory result can all belong in the same record, with their different meanings intact.

I would judge that record by whether it helps a reviewer ask a better next question. A concise dashboard is useful when the evidence beneath it remains accessible and the team can explain what the colour does not convey.
