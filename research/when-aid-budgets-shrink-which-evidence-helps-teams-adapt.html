When aid budgets shrink, which evidence helps teams adapt?

Funding changes are also changes to assumptions, risks and delivery.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Funding statistics do not measure programme quality or waste.
  • A useful record shows what changed when resources changed.
  • Software’s contribution must be tested within the work it affects.

OECD's preliminary figures put 2025 ODA from DAC members and associates at US$174.3 billion, a 23.1% decline in real terms from 2024. These are preliminary funding statistics, not a measure of programme quality or wasted money. OECD, April 2026

The figures raise a practical question for programme leaders: when resources change, what evidence is available to decide what to continue, adapt, transfer or stop?

ICAI's 2026 review of UK ODA target management highlights problems associated with budget uncertainty and attention to spending targets over agreed results. Its findings concern a particular funding system, but illustrate why the relationship between money, decisions and outcomes deserves a visible record. ICAI, March 2026 review

For an illustrative programme facing a reduction, a useful record would show which activities changed, which assumptions no longer hold, what risks increased and what evidence supports the remaining approach. It should preserve the consequences of a decision as well as the decision itself.

MatOc could support that record by linking approved updates and changes to intended outcomes. It would still leave resource allocation and programme judgement with the people responsible for them.

Product claims need restraint in this setting. Software cannot replace withdrawn services, specialist expertise or sustained funding. Its contribution must be demonstrated within the work it actually affects.

The proposition worth testing is whether clearer evidence helps teams use their available time more effectively and explain the consequences of programme choices. That is a concrete contribution against which MatOc can be assessed.
