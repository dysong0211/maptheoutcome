Is your programme record useful before a report is due?

The record should help with this week’s question too.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • A programme archive should support decisions during implementation.
  • Usefulness is stronger evidence of value than record volume.
  • Pilots should examine the practical questions teams actually ask.

I would want a programme record to earn its place in an ordinary working week. It should help someone understand a change, locate the basis for a claim or see which important question still needs an answer.

That is the standard I would set for MatOc. If its value appears only when a reporting deadline arrives, we should ask whether the product is supporting programme intelligence as fully as intended.

Consider an illustrative weekly review. The team might need to know which new evidence affects an intended outcome, which change requires attention and whether a previously reported risk remains unresolved. An appropriate view would bring those items together with sources and dates.

Freshness matters to that design. A record should distinguish when something happened, when it was reported and when someone reviewed it. A recently uploaded document may describe an old event. A recent observation may still be awaiting confirmation.

I would also keep unresolved questions visible rather than allowing them to disappear beneath newer updates. The programme lead should be able to decide whether to investigate, defer or close them, with a reason recorded where it matters.

These are proposed product principles, not claims about capabilities already proven in use. A pilot should establish which questions teams actually ask and whether the record helps them answer those questions with reasonable effort.

The ambition is a shared resource that improves understanding throughout implementation. Reporting and evaluation would then draw on a record the team has already found useful while doing the work.

About this perspective

This is a product perspective or illustrative discussion, not an empirical finding. Its recommendations are propositions for readers to consider and test.
