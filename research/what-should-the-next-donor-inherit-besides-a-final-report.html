What should the next donor inherit besides a final report?

Pass on the useful knowledge, including the unfinished questions.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Continuity needs evidence, context and practical responsibilities.
  • A new funding cycle should be able to inspect earlier learning.
  • Access and relevance must be checked before material is reused.

Imagine a programme continuing under a new funding agreement. The team may have good reasons to revise the approach, but it should be able to examine the experience already accumulated before deciding what comes next.

I would want the incoming decision-makers to understand the programme's important assumptions, changes, results and unresolved questions. That does not mean giving every funder unrestricted access to every document. It means preparing a useful, authorised account with clear boundaries.

A practical transition record could explain which delivery arrangements are established, which still depend on temporary support and which lessons are supported by evidence. It could also identify what needs further investigation before expansion.

Ownership should be decided deliberately. Who maintains the record between grants? Which partners can access it? What can be shared outside the organisation? How will someone distinguish a current finding from an earlier interpretation?

These questions belong in the design of MatOc's longer-term programme memory. A record should remain usable through changes in funding while respecting the permissions and responsibilities attached to its sources.

I would also preserve the limits of the handover. If important records are missing or a claim cannot be checked, the new team should be able to see that immediately. A polished transfer should not create an impression of certainty that the evidence does not justify.

The value of continuity is the opportunity to make the next decision with a clearer understanding of the work already done. Each new funding cycle should be able to build on an inspectable history, rather than relying on an assumption that the relevant knowledge will somehow travel with it.
