What changed in the way we define success?

A revised target should keep the explanation for the revision.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Success criteria can change during implementation.
  • The previous definition remains relevant to accountability.
  • A record should show when and why expectations changed.

A programme may discover that an original indicator is impractical, poorly defined or insufficient for understanding the change it seeks. Revising it can be reasonable. The earlier definition and the reason for revision should remain available.

Vivalt's study of development impact evaluations examines patterns of statistical significance and how they vary across methods and publication status. The analysis concerns research reporting, not the prevalence of changing targets in aid programmes. It provides a useful reason to pay attention to how the selection and presentation of results shape an evidence base. Vivalt, 2019; author manuscript

My practical argument is that a reader should be able to distinguish a criterion established before delivery from one introduced after results were observed.

Imagine a programme originally intending to measure continued service use. If it later reports only initial registrations, the record should explain the change and the question that remains unanswered. Registration may still be informative, but it does not automatically answer the earlier question.

MatOc should preserve versions of intended outcomes, measures and targets where those versions matter to interpretation. An approved revision should show who made the decision, why and what effect it has on comparisons over time.

The same principle applies to unexpected achievements. They deserve attention, but should be identified as emerging findings rather than silently replacing the original objectives.

A transparent history allows a programme to learn and adapt while making its final account easier to assess. The reader can see what was promised, what changed and which conclusions the available evidence supports.
