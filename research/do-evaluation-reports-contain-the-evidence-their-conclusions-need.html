Do evaluation reports contain the evidence their conclusions need?

A study of 37 global health evaluations found a gap between relevant questions and robust evidence.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • Raifman and colleagues assessed 37 randomly selected evaluations from five funders.
  • The study identified weaknesses in data and methods, despite relevant questions.
  • Better source capture can support review; it cannot replace sound evaluation design.

An evaluation can ask the right question and still lack the evidence needed to answer it convincingly. That distinction matters when we describe the need for better programme records.

What the study examined

Raifman and colleagues assessed the methodological quality of 37 randomly selected programme evaluations from five major global health funders. Two researchers rated relevance, validity and reliability, resolving disagreements together. Most evaluations asked relevant questions, but the authors identified substantial weaknesses in the data and methods used. Raifman et al., 2018, Journal of Development Effectiveness

What this does not establish

The assessment concerns this sample and its historical setting. It is not a current failure rate for all aid or UN programmes. It does not show that the evaluated programmes achieved nothing or that an evidence-capture tool would have corrected their methods.

Why the distinction matters for MatOc

Our product interpretation is that source availability and methodological quality should be treated as separate requirements. A programme history could help a reviewer locate observations, inspect timing and understand changes. It cannot create a valid comparison group or a reliable measure that was never collected.

A useful pilot should therefore ask two questions: can the reader find the material behind a conclusion, and is that material sufficient for the conclusion being drawn? The second question may require further data or independent evaluation expertise.

The strongest programme record makes that boundary clear. It allows a reviewer to see what is available, what the record can support and what remains unresolved.
