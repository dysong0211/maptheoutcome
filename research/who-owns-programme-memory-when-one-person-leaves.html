Who owns programme memory when one person leaves?

Shared knowledge should not depend on one person’s inbox.

MMap the OutcomeEditorial desk · Updated 9 September 2026

Key takeaway

  • A usable record should be accessible to the responsible team.
  • Collective memory needs context as well as stored documents.
  • Ownership and permissions are part of a useful handover.

Imagine a twenty-person programme team. Each person contributes a different part of the work: a meeting note, a field observation, a procurement explanation, a partner update or an interpretation of emerging results.

The programme's shared understanding should be able to outlast any one person's involvement. That does not require collecting everything on everyone's computer. It requires an agreed place for the material the team needs to understand its work.

This is the scope I would set for MatOc. Begin with authorised shared documentation and deliberate contributions. Preserve who supplied the information, what it concerns and which source supports it. Give people clear control over what enters the shared record.

Attribution matters because different people may understand the same event differently. A collective record should allow those views to be compared without silently merging them into a single confident account.

Ownership matters too. The organisation should be able to export its approved record, maintain access through staff changes and correct a mistake without losing the revision history. Sensitive information needs appropriate boundaries even when it could help explain a programme result.

I would judge the system by whether someone joining the team can understand an important decision and find the people or evidence needed to explore it further.

The purpose is a durable shared understanding. A programme should retain the knowledge it has collectively chosen to preserve, with enough context for others to use it responsibly and enough clarity for contributors to know what they are sharing.
